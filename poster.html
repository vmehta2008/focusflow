The whole idea

You lose more time deciding than studying

Every one of those is a memory problem, not a motivation problem. Software is very good at memory.

Getting the most out of it

Six habits, and it runs itself

Open the app once a day

This is the big one. The bot only knows what the app last told it, and the app tells it when you open it. Every vague answer it ever gives traces back to this. It will admit when its information is stale rather than bluff.

Put every chapter in

All of them. Paste the syllabus straight in — one per line, subject on its own line. The app is only as clever as its list; an empty backlog leaves it with nothing to suggest but a timer.

Record every score, especially the bad ones

A 40% you hid is a chapter that never gets revisited. Bad marks are the most useful data the app receives — they're how it finds what's costing you.

Answer the morning check-in

Four taps in bed. It's the difference between a diary and a coach — a diary records what happened; a coach changes what happens next.

Tell it about your gaps

Coaching, travel, dinner, prayer, the hour you need to settle afterwards. A plan that ignores your real day is one you fail before you start — and then blame yourself for.

Press 💾 every couple of weeks

Your data lives in this browser and nowhere else. A browser clean-up would take all of it, and there's no server holding a copy. Two seconds, and the app will nag you if you forget.

Honestly

Three things it won't do

It won't study for you

It removes the deciding, the forgetting and the guessing. The sitting down is still entirely yours.

It won't always be right

That's why two models check each other, and why disagreements are shown rather than quietly resolved.

It won't hold your data

No cloud backup by default. That's the trade for nobody else ever having it.

Open it. Answer five questions. Start a block.

That's the whole beginning. Everything else can wait until you want it.
